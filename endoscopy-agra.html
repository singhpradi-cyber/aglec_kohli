PROCEDURE CONSULTATION

Know what is being advised

Ask which procedure is appropriate, where it will take place, the preparation required and the expected charges.

Upper GI endoscopy

Examination of the food pipe, stomach and first part of the small intestine, with diagnostic or therapeutic care as advised.

Colonoscopy

Examination of the colon to assess bowel symptoms or investigate findings when clinically indicated.

Sigmoidoscopy

Examination of the rectum and lower part of the colon. The doctor advises whether this or a colonoscopy is appropriate.

Balloon endoscopy

Also called balloon-assisted enteroscopy, this procedure allows a closer examination of the small intestine beyond the reach of a standard upper GI endoscopy.

ERCP

A specialist endoscopic procedure for selected bile-duct and pancreatic-duct problems, based on individual assessment.

Procedure & report review

Bring previous endoscopy, colonoscopy and biopsy reports. Discuss findings, preparation, follow-up and any further care required.

PREPARATION MATTERS

Get instructions for your procedure

Follow the preparation and fasting instructions given by the procedure team. Tell them about your medicines, allergies and medical conditions. Do not stop prescribed medicines on your own.

If sedation is planned, ask about an escort, transport and restrictions afterwards. Confirm the procedure location, availability and charges before your visit.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Before you book

Will I have a procedure during my first consultation?

A consultation does not guarantee a same-day procedure. The doctor first assesses whether it is appropriate, and reception confirms availability, location and preparation.

Should I stop medicines before endoscopy?

Do not stop prescribed medicines on your own. Tell the procedure team what you take and follow the specific instructions they give you.

What should I bring?

Bring your current medicines or medicine list, earlier consultations, endoscopy or colonoscopy reports, biopsy reports and relevant blood tests.
